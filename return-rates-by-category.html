Return Rates by Category

Published by DPJ Wholesale. Return rate is a cost of goods, not a customer-service metric, and it decides which categories a small retailer can afford to carry.

The one-line version

A category returning 20% of units needs a materially different margin than one returning 4%. Most assortment advice treats returns as an afterthought; in our order data it is one of the strongest predictors of whether a line survives two seasons.

Fit is the deciding variable

Returns cluster around a single question: could the customer have known the size before ordering? Where the answer is no — apparel, footwear — returns are highest and the economics worst. Where sizing is forgiving or irrelevant — mats, pads, bands, totes — returns are lowest.

What reorders despite a high return rate

Almost nothing. High-return categories can still be profitable, but they need either a price point that absorbs the return or a fit guide good enough to cut the rate. Both are real work, and a small retailer usually only has budget for one or two such categories.

The practical rule we use

  • Proven reorders — SKUs the same account bought more than once. Buy deep; the repeat is the forecast.
  • First-time SKUs — buy narrow and wide: one case per variant, many variants. That buys information, not inventory.
  • Seasonal and fashion — a bet with a deadline. The price after the season boundary is the real price.

Stop-stocking rule

Stop a SKU when its reorder rate falls below the cost of the shelf space it occupies — not when it stops selling entirely. A slow mover that never reorders is consuming capacity a proven repeat line would use better.
